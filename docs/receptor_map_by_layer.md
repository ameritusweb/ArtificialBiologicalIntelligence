# Receptor Map: Organized by Layer

Trunk entries are near-permanent. Branch entries are stable within an environment. Canopy entries carry a shelf-life — they depend on evolutionary history and environmental configuration.

## Layer 0: Trunk (49 receptors)

The irreducible common ground. These emerge in any structured environment. 18 are invariant across all 80 generations of deep time. Every organism shares these; every Umwelt refines them.

**Sensory:** pain, fatigue, stress_detection, quantity_detection, change_detection, coincidence_detection, environmental_change_detection, staged_processing, stage_prediction

**Spatial/Temporal:** spatial_association, temporal_association, relational_observation, pattern_recognition, categorical_compression, org_boundary_detection, controllability, response_recognition, capability_change_detection

**Stability:** Mean presence 55.1/80 generations. Churn rate 0.237 per receptor per generation.

**Human mapping:** Spelke's core knowledge (objects, agents, number, space). Present in infants and across species. These are the cognitive universals — the axes that don't rotate.

## Layer 1: Branch (65 receptors)

Require trunk receptors as prerequisites. Stable within an environment but may differ between environments.

**Causal:** causal_association, causal_inference, probabilistic_causation, precedence_detection, coincidence_detection

**Social:** behavioral_prediction, predictability_mismatch, empathy, intention_recognition, belief_attribution, self_model_applied_to_others, instruction_detection, ownership_boundary

**Compression:** chunking, concept_formation, concept_grounding, compression_gain, categorical_compression

**Epistemic:** belief_detection, doubt_detection, conflation, relative_truth, thought_type_detection

**Agency:** agency_salience, self_model

**Stability:** Mean presence 55.5/80 generations. Churn rate 0.253 per receptor per generation.

**Human mapping:** Theory of mind (age 4), causal reasoning (age 5-7), basic categorization. These develop in early-to-middle childhood and are robust across cultures, though their specific expression varies.

## Layer 2: Canopy (86 receptors)

Require trunk + branch prerequisites. The biography layer — depends on evolutionary history, cultural context, environmental demands. This is where two organisms in the same environment diverge based on their different paths.

**Deep Causal:** counterfactual_reasoning, causal_chains, causal_graph_reasoning, hidden_confounder_detection, multiple_hypotheses, intervention_planning

**Deep Social:** theory_of_mind, nested_theory_of_mind, social_learning, cultural_transmission, deception_detection, moral_reasoning, perspective_taking

**Deep Compression:** mental_model, language_grounding, analogy, hierarchical_abstraction, bias_as_compression, constraint_shape, shaped_absence, missing_piece_located

**Deep Epistemic:** counterfactual_salience, fundamental_distinction, topology_awareness, absolute_truth, epistemic_strategy

**Mathematics:** exhaustive_search, necessity_detection, proof_structure, formal_composition

**Self-Modification:** developmental_trajectory, identity_continuity, metamorphic_planning, niche_construction, deliberate_complexification

**Stability:** Mean presence 45.5/80 generations. Churn rate 18.7 receptors change state per generation (total, not per-receptor). This is where the knapsack actively trades.

**Human mapping:** Formal reasoning (adolescence+), cultural-specific cognitive tools (statistical thinking, scientific method, mathematical proof), specialized expertise. Most humans never acquire the deepest entries. Institutions and cultural transmission are required for reliable induction.

## The Shelf-Life Gradient

| Layer | Stability | Human equivalent | Shelf-life |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|------------|
| Trunk | 69% present | Core knowledge | Permanent (evolutionary timescale) |
| Branch | 69% present | Childhood acquisitions | Cultural epoch (centuries) |
| Canopy | 57% present | Expert/institutional knowledge | Generational (decades) |

The map should be read with this gradient in mind. Trunk entries are claims about all minds. Branch entries are claims about minds in rich-enough environments. Canopy entries are claims about specific minds in specific contexts — and they expire.
